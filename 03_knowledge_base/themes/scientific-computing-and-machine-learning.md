# Scientific computing and machine learning

## Central theme: preserving scientific meaning through computational interfaces

The extracted corpus reveals a recurring connection between mathematical models, scientific data structures and executable workflows. A physical state must retain its meaning in an optimization model; a mesh must retain topology and sample identity in graph tensors; stochastic bases and coefficient samples must retain their relationship to a discretized operator; a validation result must stay associated with the model, data and procedure that produced it. These are different applications of a shared engineering problem: computational representations should preserve the scientific relationships they are intended to express.

This theme draws primarily on the implemented control, graph-learning and validation-workflow contributions, interpreted alongside the pilot's analytical and supplementary knowledge. Experience identities and common limitations remain in the [registry](../experiences.yaml); detailed formulations stay in the linked concepts rather than being duplicated here.

## Scientific structure guides both modeling and learned representations

[Impedance-aware NMPC](../concepts/nonlinear-model-predictive-control.md) incorporates known feedback behavior into prediction. [Geometry-conditioned message passing](../concepts/geometry-conditioned-message-passing.md) incorporates node geometry, adjacency and geometric edge information into a learned pressure-field surrogate. One uses explicit dynamics and online constrained optimization; the other learns a field mapping through encoder–message-passing–decoder computation. Their connection is a modeling principle, **not an assertion that the GNN satisfies the governing equations**: the chosen representation should expose relevant structure to the computation.

This matters in concrete ways. Quaternion representation and reference-sign alignment affect whether the control cost expresses a meaningful attitude discrepancy. Cell-dual surface graphs and imported airfoil point graphs expose different geometric entities; confusing them would change what node features and edges mean. A shared ability to reason about representation is therefore visible across physical-state modeling and scientific ML, while their mathematical guarantees remain separate. The demonstrated activities are captured by the [control-design](../competencies/impedance-aware-control-design.md) and [scientific-ML software](../competencies/scientific-ml-software.md) competencies.

The analytical [projection](../concepts/hilbert-projection.md) and supplementary [projected-evaluation](../concepts/projected-policy-evaluation.md) material supplies a further conceptual perspective: representations and approximation criteria are choices, not neutral containers. This helps articulate why preserving geometry or choosing a weighted error is consequential. It does not establish a historical implementation of projected RL or a mathematical equivalence between projection and a GNN.

## Numerical correctness includes the identity of the data

The mathematical counterpart is now more explicit in [SVD/PCA](../concepts/matrix-low-rank-pca.md), [covariance-field representations](../concepts/random-fields-sampling.md) and [stochastic Galerkin](../concepts/stochastic-pde-discretization.md). A principal component, a covariance eigenmode and a graph latent feature are all representations, but their objectives and guarantees differ. SVD truncation controls a specified reconstruction norm; KL truncation controls a field-variance tail; learned graph features support a trained mapping and do not inherit either guarantee. This is a useful way to relate numerical linear algebra and scientific ML without equating them. [FDA evidence](../evidence/ma4800.yaml); [UQ evidence](../evidence/ma5348.yaml).

[Graph-neighborhood batching](../concepts/graph-neighborhood-batching.md) makes this principle explicit. Replicating a subgraph over operating conditions requires connectivity offsets that keep copies disjoint. Supervising a subset of nodes requires global target indices and local seed indices to refer to the same physical locations. The documented `[B,N,F]` view, subset restriction and matching seed selection show that correct array dimensions alone are insufficient: the arrays must still describe the same samples and nodes. This is attributable implementation work, recorded in `ma8102:disjoint-graph-injection` and `ma8102:seed-target-alignment` in the [pyLowOrder evidence](../evidence/ma8102.yaml).

The same representational discipline appears at a different scale in [NGValidation's workflow organization](../concepts/reproducible-validation-workflows.md): the relevant unit is model × dataset × diagnostic template, with associated configurations, reports and appendix outputs. Here the preserved relationship is between an experiment definition and its artifacts, rather than between graph indices and supervised targets. The synthesis identifies a transferable software pattern—maintaining correspondence across transformations—without claiming that the two projects share an implementation.

Quaternion sign/SLERP conventions and clock-augmented reference dynamics expose another interface risk: a numerically valid array can still encode the wrong attitude or time. The thesis's configuration-driven MuJoCo batteries and state/solver/progress logs connect these interfaces to experiment inspection. Case Studies' model/config/metric layers offer a related modular design, with narrower personal benchmark-integration contributions. This is a common scientific-software pattern across distinct systems, not shared code or identical physical guarantees.

## Sampling, reproducibility and statistical adequacy are related but distinct

The [seeded data-sampling utility](../concepts/reproducible-data-sampling.md) and the graph loader both select subsets, but they answer different questions. Random row sampling changes which records enter a workload; graph seed selection determines which nodes are supervised while retaining neighborhood context. An RNG seed and a graph seed node are not the same concept.

The user's NGValidation contributions make sampling parameters and run artifacts explicit, while the pyLowOrder contributions preserve data identity during graph preparation. Together they demonstrate practical data/workflow engineering. They do not establish that a reduced dataset preserves an operating domain, that every environment reproduces identical results, or that vectorization achieved a measured speedup. Those are separate statistical or experimental questions. [Validation-workflow competency](../competencies/validation-data-workflows.md); [NGValidation contribution evidence](../evidence/scientific_paper.yaml).

## Scientific evaluation is part of the computational interpretation

### Sampling design, approximation and validation answer different questions

[Concentration and random embeddings](../concepts/concentration-random-embeddings.md) supplies a precise caution: a fixed-vector or finite-set probability bound is not a uniform guarantee over the ambient space. Similarly, a seeded subsample is reproducible but not automatically statistically adequate. [Multilevel estimators](../concepts/multilevel-multifidelity-estimation.md) reduce variance by shared-input coupling, not simply by choosing a repeatable seed. Their physical/stochastic approximation budgets are distinct from NGValidation's residual-based prediction intervals and from a GNN's learned prediction error. The corpus supports recognizing these differences; no MC calibration of those projects is claimed.

Stochastic Galerkin's physical/stochastic multi-indices, sparse Kronecker factors and matrix-free `pcg` examples also expose a reusable computational pattern: preserve mathematical factorization instead of flattening it into an opaque array. That pattern complements the user's implemented graph-index and artifact contracts, while the MATLAB PDE programs remain teaching resources. The distinct personal contribution is the P2 explanation linking FEM regularity to MLMC work, represented by the [uncertainty/error-analysis competency](../competencies/uncertainty-error-analysis.md).

[Sensitivity and rare events](../concepts/sensitivity-rare-event-estimation.md) broaden the evaluation vocabulary: which inputs explain variance, and how costly is estimating a very small failure probability? Neither a good average prediction nor a low reconstruction error answers those questions. Engineering-surrogate and reliability applications are plausible transfers of this combined knowledge, not additional demonstrated deployments.

The pilot complements implementation with the ability to distinguish what an output means. [Interval aggregation](../concepts/prediction-interval-aggregation.md) preserves the NGValidation paper's intersection and the code's widest-interval selection as distinct procedures. This is useful methodological knowledge for discussing uncertainty-aware systems; individual authorship of the aggregation code is not established. Similarly, the [recovery lesson](../concepts/progress-triggered-recovery.md) separates a functioning predictive controller from successful task completion, within the thesis's reported simulation and classification limits.

For aerodynamic ML, the external DLR experiment supplies scientific motivation for geometry-conditioned pressure prediction, but its error values are not the user's achieved pyLOM metrics. The personal evidence establishes implementation and correctness-oriented integration, not a reproduced accuracy or throughput benchmark. These examples encourage interpretation of results in terms of their method, data, objective and evaluation protocol rather than treating every reported number as an interchangeable achievement. [pyLowOrder result boundary](../evidence/ma8102.yaml); [thesis result and lesson](../evidence/ma6018.yaml).

## Transferable capabilities and technical interpretation

### Choosing a metric that matches the scientific question

The personally implemented [Hausdorff contour metric](../concepts/geometric-field-validation.md) adds a concrete validation capability: worst nearest-point discrepancy complements average Chamfer error, occupancy IoU and scalar regression loss. Grid/threshold/empty-surface behavior remain part of the metric's contract. An accurate boundary and a faster feasible trajectory are distinct outcomes, just as logging a numerical success flag does not validate the thesis classifier or NGValidation coverage. These connections strengthen the existing [validation/data-workflow competency](../competencies/validation-data-workflows.md) with metric implementation and simulation automation, not invented certification.

The source-qualified findings also reveal a transferable experimental habit: separate representation changes, initialization, solver cost and outcome criteria before interpreting an improvement. Boundary-focused training can help one geometry; finer decomposition can increase cost without clear objective gain; more predictive detail can leave task success unchanged. These are different experiments, not a universal theorem about ML or optimization. Their value is a disciplined vocabulary for designing and interpreting comparisons. [Case Studies evidence](../evidence/cit413042.yaml); [thesis evidence](../evidence/ma6018.yaml).

Taken together, the demonstrated work supports capabilities in **scientific model integration**, **geometry-aware ML implementation**, **tensor/index reasoning**, and **configurable data/reporting workflows**. The mathematical foundations add the ability to discuss approximation choices and assumptions; they are not merely supporting keywords for software skills. The supplementary Bellman/projection material expands the conceptual framework for sequential learning without implying personal implementation of those algorithms.

The resulting technical pattern is an applied-mathematics and scientific-computing orientation in which machine learning is connected to engineering representations, numerical interfaces and interpretable evaluation. Practical strength appears in making formal methods usable through existing numerical/ML libraries while retaining the meaning of states, targets and artifacts. Operator, low-rank and UQ theory adds substantive analytical breadth, including a documented research-method presentation. The evidence supports this pattern more directly than claims of production scale, hardware deployment or benchmark superiority.

Potential transfer includes engineering-surrogate tooling, differentiable planning/model interfaces, scientific ML pipelines and model-evaluation software. Further applications to safety-critical deployment, distributed learning or quantitative-risk systems would need their own evidence. All 24 source groups now map 25 reviewed documents. Source-profile coverage is complete; global semantic consolidation and independent reproduction of project outcomes have not been performed.
