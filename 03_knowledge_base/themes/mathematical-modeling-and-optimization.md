# Mathematical modeling and optimization

## Central theme: making the mathematical problem match the intended task

The extracted corpus brings together analytical knowledge of operators, approximation and uncertainty with practical formulation of constrained dynamical problems. Its unifying concern is not simply finding an optimizer: it is choosing a state, representation, error measure and update rule that preserve what the problem means. This connects functional analysis, numerical uncertainty quantification, approximate dynamic programming, compliant robot control and statistical uncertainty interpretation.

The connections below are thematic interpretations of existing knowledge and evidence, not claims that every mathematical connection was historically used in a project. The Case Studies course/project documents describe one experience.

## Foundations: solvability, approximation and the geometry of error

[Neumann-series inversion](../concepts/neumann-series.md) illustrates a disciplined starting point: a bounded linear operator on a Banach space with norm below one admits the represented series construction for its inverse. The hypotheses belong to the method. Knowing this construction supplies a way to reason about operator equations and repeated approximation, independently of whether a personal solver implementation exists.

[Hilbert projection](../concepts/hilbert-projection.md) asks a complementary question: what is the best approximation within a chosen set, measured in a specified inner-product geometry? This makes the choice of approximation space and norm mathematically substantive. The supplementary [projected-policy-evaluation](../concepts/projected-policy-evaluation.md) material extends that concern to value approximation: stationary-distribution weighting, Bellman error and projected Bellman error are distinct constructions, and the represented norm-mismatch counterexample warns against transferring convergence intuition without its assumptions. These connections are supported by [Functional Analysis knowledge](../evidence/ma3001.yaml) and [ADPRL knowledge](../evidence/tum-approximate-dynamic-programming.yaml), not by invented implementation experience.

A further connection is to the weighting of tracking and control effort in an optimal-control objective. A Hilbert projection, a weighted value approximation and a quadratic tracking cost are **not the same algorithm**. What they share is that the representation and measure of error determine which deviations matter. This is a useful analytical lens for discussing approximation quality, control priorities and the limits of a numerical answer; it is not a convergence theorem for the combined systems.

## From future costs to an executable control formulation

[Constrained nonlinear optimization](../concepts/constrained-nonlinear-optimization.md) sharpens the distinction between writing constraints and obtaining a meaningful solver step. Tangent geometry, constraint qualifications, KKT and critical-cone curvature explain when a local stationarity system is justified. SQP can face an infeasible linearization even when the nonlinear problem is feasible; elastic restoration and penalty formulations address a specific numerical obstruction rather than changing “optimization” into an undifferentiated tool. This educational knowledge complements the thesis's implemented SQP-based control stack, but does not establish its constraint qualifications, global convergence or closed-loop safety. [MA3503 evidence](../evidence/ma3503.yaml).

[Stationary-policy Bellman evaluation](../concepts/bellman-policy-evaluation.md) and [nonlinear model predictive control](../concepts/nonlinear-model-predictive-control.md) both organize decisions through future consequences, but at different levels. The former describes costs under a policy through a finite-state transition operator. The thesis formulates a finite-horizon trajectory problem with dynamics, tracking and input penalties, an initial-state condition and feedforward acceleration bounds, then applies the first action and replans. Supplementary knowledge of policy/value iteration complements this understanding; it does not make the thesis an implemented RL system.

The distinctive practical modeling step is including impedance feedback **inside the prediction dynamics**. The predicted body already has a compliant response; the optimized variable is feedforward acceleration rather than the complete physical wrench. Thus the formulation makes explicit both what optimization can choose and what the surrounding feedback law contributes. CasADi/acados multiple shooting, SQP/Gauss–Newton, implicit Runge–Kutta integration and HPIPM connect this formulation to numerical execution. The multirate decision—holding feedforward while recomputing feedback at physics substeps—shows that a mathematically specified controller still requires an execution model consistent with its feedback structure. These are attributable formulation and implementation activities, captured by the [control-design competency](../competencies/impedance-aware-control-design.md) and [thesis claims](../evidence/ma6018.yaml).

## Internal objectives and external success are different questions

### Planning a trajectory and making execution succeed

Case Studies adds an implemented team example of [direct transcription](../concepts/optimal-control-transcription.md): a geometric RRT seed is refined through Euler state defects, finite-footprint distance constraints and IPOPT. The thesis uses a different combination—implicit-RK multiple shooting with compliant feedback inside prediction—and applies only the first action before replanning. Both translate a continuous physical objective into a finite constrained problem; integration scheme, admissible controls, geometry and initialization determine what that finite problem actually represents. Neither the report's “multiple shooting” label nor a solver-success flag erases these distinctions.

[Geometric planning](../concepts/geometric-sampling-motion-planning.md) and [feedback architectures](../concepts/state-feedback-observers.md) broaden the theoretical frame: configuration-space connectivity, observation, disturbance rejection and internal/zero dynamics concern different parts of the loop. IN2138 supplies accredited syllabus knowledge; Advanced Control supplies supplementary pole-placement/LQR/observer/linearization content, not a personally implemented LQR or SLAM system. These foundations help explain why a path, a state estimate and a controller solve different problems without turning conceptual exposure into a software achievement.

The distinctive implemented thesis step is combining [impedance/contact models](../concepts/impedance-contact-dynamics.md) with task-progress feedback. A smooth local elastic predictor can be well integrated yet fail to improve insertion; nearest-path geometry can help detect stalls yet perform poorly as a clock. Thus the useful methodological capability is to assign each model the right job and test its effect, rather than assuming one sufficiently detailed predictor will supply every missing robustness property. [Personal control capability](../competencies/impedance-aware-control-design.md); [thesis comparisons](../evidence/ma6018.yaml).

### What counts as success?

An optimization objective measures the modeled preference; it does not by itself detect whether assembly is progressing. [Progress-triggered recovery](../concepts/progress-triggered-recovery.md) introduces a second loop that observes swept path progress and changes the reference when progress stalls. The reported comparison suggests that, in this setup, adding prediction detail did not supply the robustness benefit that corrective reference search provided.

The thesis reports 47/121 successful baseline cases versus 78/121 with discrete spiral recovery on a fixed planar-offset simulation grid. These remain reported, unreproduced outcomes: the intended final-position criterion is below 2.5 mm, while saved classification used a different 12 mm minimum-over-time rule. The result therefore informs a scoped comparison, not an independently established success guarantee. Its conditions and limits are retained in `ma6018:spiral-grid-result` and `ma6018:recovery-versus-prediction` in the [thesis evidence](../evidence/ma6018.yaml).

The same distinction between a procedure's internal rule and its intended meaning appears in [prediction-interval aggregation](../concepts/prediction-interval-aggregation.md). Intersecting uncertainty intervals and selecting the widest constituent are different mathematical operations, neither automatically providing the claimed coverage property. Together, these examples motivate a transferable habit: ask which property a computation actually establishes, and which task-level property still needs separate evaluation. The NGValidation comparison is existing scientific knowledge, not evidence that the user individually authored its aggregation routine. [Paper/code evidence](../evidence/scientific_paper.yaml).

Case Studies supplies another example: [distance-field quality](../concepts/geometric-field-validation.md) can improve while derivative cost rises and the attained path objective barely changes. Sampled footprint constraints are not continuous collision certificates; a geometric spline seed is not necessarily dynamically feasible. Similarly, the thesis's sampled-region area observation and unavailable-information oracles are diagnostics, not deployable success guarantees. These comparisons connect modeling, numerical approximation and experimental interpretation while retaining the independent source conditions.

## Approximation becomes an explicit error–work problem

The [Sobolev/weak formulation](../concepts/sobolev-distributions.md) and [random-PDE discretization](../concepts/stochastic-pde-discretization.md) give a concrete bridge from abstract analysis to engineering simulation. A lognormal coefficient is positive pathwise, yet lacks a uniform ellipticity bound over all realizations. Tracking random regularity/constants is therefore part of obtaining an error estimate, not a technical footnote. The seminar's FEM rate then controls estimator bias and coupled-difference variance: approximation quality changes the economically useful sample allocation. The personally attributed P2 presentation documents analysis and explanation of this bridge; it does not establish personal solver implementation. [Analytical competency](../competencies/uncertainty-error-analysis.md); [seminar evidence](../evidence/ma6015.yaml).

[Multilevel/multifidelity estimation](../concepts/multilevel-multifidelity-estimation.md) makes optimization itself the object of uncertainty analysis: choose meshes, levels, error splits and sample counts under a work/tolerance budget. Correlated coarse models are valuable because of their correction variance and cost, not because they are merely cheaper. BLUE/SAOB generalizes grouping but adds covariance/allocation complexity. In parallel, [convex conjugacy and sparsity](../concepts/convex-conjugacy-sparse-optimization.md) shows how geometry and dual norms determine feasible sparse-recovery formulations. These are two distinct uses of optimization—resource allocation and model reconstruction—connected by explicit objectives, constraints and applicability assumptions.

The common analytical pattern is to separate **existence**, **approximation quality**, **computational work**, and **success in the intended application**. Weak compactness can establish a minimizer without an efficient algorithm; a good discretization does not eliminate Monte Carlo variance; an efficient estimator does not validate the underlying physical model. The thesis and NGValidation add practical examples of the last distinction. This connection is a synthesis of supported methods, not a claim that MLMC was incorporated into either project.

## Objectives, algorithms and geometries in statistical learning

The new material makes a three-way distinction explicit: [statistical risk](../concepts/statistical-risk-model-selection.md) asks what population quantity is useful; [regularization](../concepts/regularized-linear-inference.md) chooses a stable or structured estimator; [optimization](../concepts/first-order-stochastic-optimization.md) asks how accurately and cheaply its finite objective can be solved. A fast solver cannot compensate for target leakage or select an appropriate loss on its own. Conversely, a useful statistical model can have a difficult nonconvex fitting problem.

Convex duality, [proximal/block methods](../concepts/proximal-block-optimization.md) and [minimax operators](../concepts/minimax-adversarial-learning.md) broaden the available solution vocabulary: thresholding a composite penalty, calling a linear oracle and taking an extragradient step solve different numerical problems. Smoothness, strong convexity, operator Lipschitzness and domain geometry determine which rates apply. Newton/self-concordant barrier theory supplies a curvature-based contrast, not a global guarantee for every learned constraint in the projects.

[Riemannian optimization](../concepts/nonconvex-geometric-optimization.md) and [probability-space flows](../concepts/diffusion-score-probability-flows.md) reveal a further cross-domain relationship: changing geometry changes gradients, admissible directions and convergence assumptions. Matrix-manifold optimization, a Wasserstein entropy flow, KL-constrained policy updates and a quaternion control state all use nontrivial geometry, but they are not one algorithm or historically combined implementation. In particular the thesis's quaternion coordinates do not establish use of a Riemannian solver.

EM connects latent inference to majorization; SVRG and multilevel estimation both exploit correlated corrections, but optimize/estimate different objects under different sampling assumptions. These links strengthen transferable mathematical reasoning without claiming that the neural projects implement MLMC or every taught optimizer. The distinct [inference/generative synthesis](statistical-inference-and-generative-modeling.md) develops model identity, latent structure and statistical meaning rather than absorbing them all into solver theory.

## Discrete structure and inference extend the formulation vocabulary

[Matroid greedy structure](../concepts/graph-greedy-matroid-optimization.md), [residual flow certificates](../concepts/network-flow-cost-optimality.md) and [matching/parity reductions](../concepts/matching-assignment-routing.md) add a distinct algorithm-design perspective. Feasibility is combinatorial rather than merely a smooth constraint surface: exchange, integrality, cuts and augmenting structures determine when an efficient local update certifies a global answer. These guarantees do not transfer automatically to a nonconvex trajectory optimizer.

Language [structured inference](../concepts/structured-sequence-parsing.md) supplies another use of constrained computation: forward sums, Viterbi paths, span charts and directed dependency trees preserve different objects. A parser's directed cycle contraction is not an undirected MST, and summing possible explanations is not selecting the best one. The transferable theoretical reasoning is to identify structure before choosing a solver or approximation.

NGValidation's [hull feasibility LP](../concepts/surrogate-domain-split-validation.md) gives a project-level bridge between continuous geometry and statistical use restrictions. A feasible barycentric certificate answers membership, not predictive reliability; residual/calibration analysis answers different questions. This relationship strengthens the existing objective-versus-success distinction, without claiming personal implementation of all graph or validation algorithms.

## Transferable capabilities and technical interpretation

The combination supports complementary strengths:

- **Analytical problem specification:** identify spaces, variables, admissible approximations and assumptions before interpreting an inverse, projection or iterative method. Theoretical coursework has value here without needing a software artifact.
- **Model-to-solver translation:** formulate compliant dynamics and constrained tracking, then align state representations, numerical transcription and execution timing. This is demonstrated by personal thesis work, not merely by knowledge of solver names.
- **Methodological discrimination:** distinguish approximation error from convergence, optimized cost from physical task success, and an interval operation from an uncertainty guarantee. The thesis provides a documented practical example; operator/ADPRL and NGValidation concepts broaden the reasoning vocabulary.
- **Numerical error and resource reasoning:** connect function-space regularity to FEM rates, statistical uncertainty and hierarchy allocation; explain a research argument with its assumptions intact. P2 provides documented personal exposition, with broader UQ coverage establishing the theoretical context rather than additional personal experiments.
- **Layered model and experiment reasoning:** distinguish geometric connectivity, dynamic feasibility, compliant tracking and physical task progress; combine a documented control/recovery implementation with team planning experience and specifically attributed geometry/learning contributions. A richer theoretical control vocabulary supports this interpretation without implying supplementary algorithm implementation.

The emerging technical profile is one that connects mathematical structure to numerical modeling decisions and interprets results within their actual operating assumptions. This is more specific than a generic combination of mathematics and programming: it includes deciding what must be modeled, how an approximation changes the question, and when a separate corrective or evaluative mechanism is needed.

These foundations are potentially useful in other control, simulation and quantitative-modeling settings.
